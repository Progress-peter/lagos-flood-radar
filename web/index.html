Radar finds 800–1,560 ha of flooded land in Lagos every rainy season, mostly in Epe, Ibeju‑Lekki and Badagry

Sentinel‑1 radar sees through rain clouds. Comparing each rainy season (April–October) with that year's dry season shows where ground turned to standing water, 2017–2025. Wetland and mangrove flooding is mapped separately from flooding on land.

How it was measured

Sentinel‑1A radar (ascending track 1, the only track covering all of Lagos, 12‑day revisit every year). For each year, every April–October image is compared with that year's January–March median. A 20 m cell counts as flooded when its VH backscatter drops by more than 3 dB to below −20 dB in at least two images.

What was removed

  • Permanent water (JRC Global Surface Water, ≥10 months a year) and ESA WorldCover open water
  • Bare and sand‑filled ground, which caused one‑off false detections at reclamation sites
  • Slopes above 5° and clusters smaller than 8 cells

Limits

  • Flash floods that drain within hours and water between buildings are mostly invisible to radar, so built‑up Lagos is under‑counted.
  • Exposure means people or buildings located inside detected flooding, not confirmed damage. A few small detections in dense neighbourhoods (Lagos Mainland 2025, Eti‑Osa 2024) carry most of the building counts and need ground checking.
  • 2026 is excluded: only 8 rainy‑season images so far, against 17–18 in other years.

Data: Copernicus Sentinel‑1 GRD; JRC Global Surface Water v1.4; ESA WorldCover 2021; JRC GHS‑POP R2023A; Google Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal; GRIP4 roads; FAO GAUL 2015 boundaries. Processed in Google Earth Engine by Progress Peter Akpan.
